# H4 packet dirty scheduling — independent Phase 2 adversarial critique

Review of [the written design](fbp-h4-packet-dirty-design-20261113.md), separately from implementation.

1. **Missing writer backpressure wake:** packet dequeue frees upstream publication capacity, even if the reader is unrelated to the source branch in topo order. Must mark the queue's producer on `reserve`, `take_output` and reader closure. A transport pop without mark is a liveness defect.
2. **Missing reader wake:** after successful atomic commit, every affected process reader must become eligible for selection. Mark all fanout targets, not merely the publisher.
3. **In-refresh queue mutation ordering:** full legacy refresh may reserve from a later consumer and make an earlier producer runnable but historically does not repeat unless a process finishes. Retain dirty updates to be checked at the next refresh; do not spontaneously spin a new repeated scan because a queue changes. For finish, preserve original full repeated scan.
4. **Publication pending:** publisher must be reevaluated after a target queue is popped or closed; unsuccessful Full commit should not spin. When successful publication transitions handoff, publisher must be marked for potential continuation even if there is no outgoing edge.
5. **External wake race:** a notification may arrive during the refresh. Read the wake epoch **before** scanning, never after, so a later notification remains visible to the next refresh. Wake notifications must force full scan even in a packet-driven eligible session.
6. **Zero-input, Custom, Optional:** cannot be scheduled solely from queue data. Disable dirty-path scheduling for the entire session when any such policy exists. Dynamic `WaitInput` may receive request-local closure without a packet event, and `WaitInputs` may install a Custom/Optional policy; disable incremental mode *before* refresh and permanently afterward for either staged operation.
7. **Request lifecycle and cancellation:** `close_request` can alter closure semantics, so conservatively invalidate full on seal. `cancel_request` may remove packets from queues and replace active process state; invalidate full before recovery `refresh()`. Scoped status remains passive. Avoid any cross-request pollution of dirty membership.
8. **EOS and completion:** closing incoming writers, finishing a process, and closing its outgoing writers change EOS. Mark both endpoints of touched queues; preserving full re-scan on `Finished` is the safest first implementation. Do not misclassify `Complete` as `Finished`.
9. **Ready queue ordering:** only indices marked by actual local transitions may be considered on sparse refresh, always in ascending index. The ready queue remains FIFO. If a dirty mark occurs within scanning, process it on a subsequent refresh rather than allowing recursive activation loops in the same pass.
10. **Process ownership:** on `complete` and `reject`, mark that process after its handoff transitions and before refreshing, even if no queue changed. Unsuccessful dispatch/completion must retain ownership and error paths exactly.
11. **Resource cost and fallback:** BTreeSet insertion and wake epoch Mutex checking have a cost on tiny graphs; evaluate 1/16/64/256 controls. No fixed budget or per-request constant-time claim. If 128-node reachable chain does not improve because it uses Optional, acknowledge that by design rather than claim H5 solved.
12. **Unbounded custom mutation:** code running inside a Process may only affect session through documented handles, packets and wake notifications. External plugin side effects on arbitrary objects are not scheduler inputs; do not claim general plugin lock / side-effect control.

**Conditional Phase 3 approval:** isolate the old refresh body into a single reusable function, audit every queue mutation and handoff call site, preserve full-scan fallback for any uncertain path. Add regression coverage and require final-head CI + paired performance, with code diff reviewed for all 12 items. Otherwise reject the experiment unmerged.
